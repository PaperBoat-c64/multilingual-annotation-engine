# Poetry-Aware Translation — 信达雅 Quality Standard

**Core principle**: Lyrics are not ordinary text. Translation preserves mood, imagery, metaphor, poetic rhythm, narrative voice, and cultural symbolism.

## The 信达雅 Standard

Every translation must satisfy three criteria, applied universally to ALL languages:

### 信 (Xìn) — Faithfulness
Accurate to the source meaning. No fabrication, no omission.

- Every semantic unit in the source is accounted for
- Implied information is marked, not silently inserted
- Cultural references are preserved, not erased
- Ambiguities are noted, not resolved arbitrarily

### 达 (Dá) — Expressiveness
Reads naturally to a native speaker. No translationese.

- Grammar is correct for the target language
- Word order is natural (SOV→SVO reordering applied)
- Idioms are rendered as target-language idioms (not glossed)
- Register matches the source (formal ↔ colloquial)

### 雅 (Yǎ) — Elegance
Appropriate register and poetic quality. Beautiful to read.

- Cadence and rhythm are pleasing in the target language
- Poetic devices are recreated, not explained
- Word choice considers euphony and connotation
- Line breaks preserve poetic integrity

## Preservation Dimensions

### 1. Mood (情绪 / 気分 / مود)

The emotional tone must survive translation:

| Source Mood | Must Read As | Anti-Pattern |
|-------------|-------------|--------------|
| Longing (切ない) | Melancholic, wistful | Mechanical "I am sad" |
| Joy (خوشحالی) | Buoyant, light | Flat "I am happy" |
| Defiance (反逆) | Fierce, sharp | Neutral "I disagree" |
| Devotion (إخلاص) | Tender, sincere | Clinical "I am loyal" |
| Nostalgia (nostalgia) | Warm, bittersweet | Dry "I remember" |
| Awe (敬畏) | Reverent, hushed | Casual "It's great" |

**Check**: After translating, ask: "Does this evoke the same emotional response as the original?"

### 2. Imagery (意象 / صور)

Visual and sensory metaphors must transfer:

```
Source:     月光如水 (yuèguāng rú shuǐ)
Literal:    "moonlight like water"
Bad:        "bright moonlight"
Good:       "moonlight flowing like water"
```

**Strategy**:
- Identify sensory elements (visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory)
- Reproduce the sensory quality in the target language
- If the exact metaphor doesn't work, find the target-language equivalent
- Never reduce imagery to abstract summary

### 3. Metaphor (隐喻 / استعاره)

Cultural metaphor mapping:

```
Persian:    دل من تنگ است (del-am tang ast)
Literal:    "my heart is narrow"
English:    "my heart aches" or "I feel constricted with longing"
```

**Strategy**:
- If the metaphor exists in the target culture → use the target equivalent
- If the metaphor is unique to the source culture → preserve it, add cultural note in advanced mode
- Never replace a metaphor with a bare factual statement

### 4. Poetic Rhythm (韵律 / آهنگ)

Natural cadence in the target language:

```
Japanese haiku:  5-7-5 syllable pattern
English haiku:   approximately 5-7-5 with natural English cadence
                 (not forced syllable counting)
```

**Strategy**:
- Match syllable count approximately, not mechanically
- Preserve line-internal pauses (caesuras)
- Respect the natural stress pattern of the target language
- Rhyme is secondary to rhythm — don't force rhymes at the expense of naturalness

### 5. Narrative Voice (叙事声音)

```
First-person intimate:  "I wandered lonely as a cloud"
Third-person distant:   "He wandered lonely as a cloud"
```

**Preserve**: Person, intimacy level, gender (when explicit), social register.

### 6. Cultural Symbolism (文化象征)

| Symbol | Culture | Meaning | Translation Strategy |
|--------|---------|---------|---------------------|
| 月 (moon) | Chinese | Longing, reunion, home | Preserve; may need cultural note |
| گل و بلبل (rose & nightingale) | Persian | Lover and beloved | Preserve; annotate if unfamiliar |
| 桜 (cherry blossom) | Japanese | Transience, beauty, mortality | Preserve with note or equivalent |
| قلب (heart) | Arabic | Seat of emotion and intellect | "Heart" works universally |
| 柳 (willow) | Chinese | Parting, sorrow, grace | Preserve as "willow"; "weeping willow" for Western readers |

## Language-Specific Poetic Conventions

### Japanese
- **Kigo (季語)**: Seasonal words are essential poetic markers. Preserve their seasonal association.
- **Kireji (切れ字)**: Cutting words (や, かな, けり) mark poetic pauses. Indicate with line breaks or punctuation.
- **Pillow words (枕詞)**: Fixed epithets (e.g., あしひきの for "mountain"). Translate the meaning, annotate the convention.

### Chinese
- **Parallelism (对仗)**: Couplets with matching structure. Preserve structural parallelism.
- **Allusion (典故)**: References to classical literature. Preserve with context.
- **Tone pattern (平仄)**: Level/oblique tone alternation. Cannot reproduce in non-tonal languages — compensate with stress alternation.

### Persian
- **Rhyme (قافیه)**: Essential to ghazal form. Preserve end-rhyme.
- **Refrain (ردیف)**: Repeated word/phrase at end of couplets. Preserve exactly.
- **Metaphor system**: Rose = beloved, nightingale = lover, wine = spiritual ecstasy. Preserve the symbolic system.

### Arabic
- **Monorhyme**: Entire poem rhymes on one sound. Preserve if possible; note otherwise.
- **Meter (بحر)**: Quantitative meter based on syllable weight. Indicate in advanced mode.
- **Nasīb (نسيب)**: Opening section about lost love. Mark structural convention.

### Hindi/Urdu
- **Mukhda-Antara**: Chorus-Verse structure in film songs. Preserve structure.
- **Rhyme scheme**: Typically AABB or ABAB across verses. Preserve.
- **Code-switching**: Hindi-Urdu-English mixing is intentional. Preserve the mix.

### Korean
- **Sijo (시조)**: 3-line form with 14-16 syllables per line. Preserve line structure.
- **Internal rhyme and alliteration**: Common in K-Pop lyrics. Reproduce where possible.

### Russian
- **Rhyme**: Essential to Russian poetry. Preserve end-rhyme.
- **Meter**: Syllabo-tonic (stress-based). Approximate in target language.
- **Inversion**: Poetic word order inversion. Indicate in annotation; preserve natural order in translation.

### Turkish
- **Syllable count (hece ölçüsü)**: Traditional folk poetry uses fixed syllable counts. Preserve line length.
- **Reduplication**: Common for emphasis (yavaş yavaş = slowly slowly). Preserve.

### Thai
- **Internal rhyme (กลอน)**: Complex rhyming within and between lines. Note in advanced mode.
- **Tone pattern**: Cannot reproduce in non-tonal languages. Note.

## Mandatory Quality Verification Loop

**Before finalizing any translation**, run the mandatory 7-question verification. This is NOT optional — every translation must pass all checks before being accepted.

### The 7 Questions

Ask these for EVERY line of the translation:

| # | Question | What to Check |
|---|----------|---------------|
| 1 | **Is the meaning accurate?** | Does the translation faithfully convey the source meaning? No omissions, no additions, no distortions. |
| 2 | **Is the grammar natural?** | Are word order, agreement, tense, aspect, and function words correct for the target language? No source-language grammar leaking through. |
| 3 | **Would a native speaker actually say this?** | Read it aloud (mentally). Does it sound like something a native speaker would spontaneously produce — not a translation? |
| 4 | **Is there a more idiomatic expression?** | Could a fixed phrase, collocation, or idiom in the target language express this more naturally? |
| 5 | **Is there a fixed phrase that should be used?** | For common expressions (greetings, emotions, transitions), is there a conventional phrase in the target language that should replace a literal rendering? |
| 6 | **Does the translation preserve the emotion?** | Is the emotional tone (longing, joy, melancholy, defiance, tenderness) preserved? Does it hit with the same emotional weight? |
| 7 | **Does it sound like lyrics rather than a dictionary entry?** | Is it poetic, rhythmic, singable? Or does it read like a textbook gloss? Lyrics have cadence, compression, and musicality. |

### The Loop

```
1. Translate the line
2. Ask all 7 questions
3. If ANY answer is "No" → revise the translation → go back to step 2
4. Only when ALL 7 answers are "Yes" → the translation is accepted
5. Move to the next line
```

**Repeat until all checks pass.** A translation is not complete until every line gets a "Yes" on all 7 questions. If a line requires 3 revisions, do 3 revisions. If it requires 5, do 5. Never settle for "close enough."

### Correction Procedure

When any check fails:
1. Identify which of the 7 questions returned "No" (may be multiple)
2. Diagnose the root cause — is it a word choice issue, a grammar issue, or a phrasing issue?
3. Re-generate the translation addressing the specific failures
4. Re-verify ALL 7 questions (not just the ones that failed — a fix can break something else)
5. Continue until all 7 pass

## Native-Speaker Target Language Validation

**This section provides deeper context for Questions 3–7 of the Mandatory Quality Verification Loop.** The translation must be evaluated from the perspective of a **native speaker of the target language** — never from the source language perspective.

### The Native Speaker Test (Expanded)

Beyond the 7 questions, consider these deeper native-speaker perspectives:

1. **"Would I say this?"** — Does this sound like something a native speaker would actually say, sing, or write? Or does it sound like a translation?

2. **"Is this how we express this emotion?"** — Does the emotional expression match how native speakers of the target language convey longing, joy, sadness, defiance?

3. **"Do these metaphors make sense to me?"** — Are the poetic devices natural in the target language, or do they feel foreign/translated?

4. **"Is the rhythm natural?"** — Does the cadence flow naturally in the target language? Are the stress patterns, syllable counts, and pauses appropriate?

5. **"Does the register match?"** — Is the formality level appropriate for the context in the target culture?

### Target-Language Quality Checklist

For EVERY translation, verify:

- [ ] **Grammar**: All grammatical features of the target language are correctly applied (gender, case, aspect, honorifics, particles, etc.)
- [ ] **Word order**: Natural target-language word order (SOV → SVO, topic-comment → subject-predicate, etc.)
- [ ] **Collocations**: Words combine naturally as a native speaker would combine them
- [ ] **Idioms**: Idiomatic expressions are rendered as target-language idioms (not glossed)
- [ ] **Register**: Formal/informal/casual/intimate register matches the source intent in the target language's register system
- [ ] **Cultural framing**: Cultural references are accessible to a target-language audience without losing the source flavor
- [ ] **Prosody**: Rhythm, stress, and cadence are natural for the target language
- [ ] **No calques**: No source-language structures have been loan-translated into unnatural target-language forms

### Target-Language-Specific Anti-Patterns

Examples of source-language contamination for various target languages:

**Target: Japanese**
- ❌ Using です/ます in lyrics that should be intimate (use plain form)
- ❌ Preserving English articles as unnecessary その/この
- ❌ Mechanical SOV from SVO source (should feel like natural Japanese SOV)

**Target: Arabic**
- ❌ Ignoring gender agreement throughout the text
- ❌ Using MSA (Fusha) for a pop song that should be in dialect (Ammiya)
- ❌ Preserving non-Arabic word order instead of natural VSO/SVO Arabic order

**Target: Spanish**
- ❌ Wrong ser/estar distinction
- ❌ Incorrect subjunctive mood usage
- ❌ Pronoun placement that follows source language patterns

**Target: Russian**
- ❌ Wrong verbal aspect (совершенный/несовершенный вид)
- ❌ Incorrect case assignment after prepositions
- ❌ Unnatural word order (forcing SVO when Russian allows flexible order for emphasis)

**Target: Korean**
- ❌ Wrong speech level for the context (e.g., 합쇼체 in an intimate love song)
- ❌ Unnatural particle usage from source language influence
- ❌ Incorrect honorific application

**Target: French**
- ❌ Missing or incorrect elision and liaison flow
- ❌ Wrong gender agreement on adjectives and participles
- ❌ Unnatural word order (e.g., adverb placement following English patterns)

### Validation Process

```
Source Text → Translation → Native-Speaker Quality Review → Revision → Final Output
                                ↑                                |
                                └────── Repeat until passes ────┘
```

If a native speaker would pause and think "this sounds translated" — revise it. The goal is that a native speaker reads it and thinks it was **originally written in their language**.

## Anti-Patterns: What to Avoid

### ❌ Mechanical Word-by-Word
```
Japanese:     君の名は (kimi no na wa)
Bad:          "You 's name as for"
Good:         "Your name"
```

### ❌ Erasing Poetic Devices
```
Persian:      دل من تنگ است (del-am tang ast)
Bad:          "I feel sad"
Good:         "My heart is constricted"
```

### ❌ Explaining Instead of Translating
```
Chinese:      沉鱼落雁 (chényú-luòyàn)
Bad:          "(this is a Chinese idiom meaning extreme beauty)"
Good:         "beauty that shames fish and wild geese"
```

### ❌ Preserving Source Word Order
```
Hindi:        मैं तुमसे प्यार करता हूँ (main tumse pyaar karta hoon)
              [I] [you-to] [love] [do] [am] — SOV
Bad:          "I you to love do am"
Good:         "I love you"
```

### ❌ Wrong Target Language Assumption
```
Scenario:      User requested Spanish translation, system produced English
               (because English was hardcoded as the default target)
Fix:           Always parse the user's target language specification.
               Never assume any default target language.
```

### ❌ Over-Translation
```
Simple lyric:  I love you
Bad:           "My eternal devotion resonates through the cosmos for thee"
Good:          "I love you"
```

### ❌ Under-Translation
```
Poetic lyric:  君は僕の光 (kimi wa boku no hikari)
               [you] [TOP] [my] [light]
Bad:           "You're my light" (too casual)
Good:          "You are my light" (preserves the weight)
```
